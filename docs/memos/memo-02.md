## Purpose of Memo 2

Memo 2 asks you to evaluate whether a publicly traded stock appears undervalued, overvalued, or reasonably priced based on valuation evidence.

In Memo 1, you diagnosed whether the company appeared financially healthy enough to continue analyzing. Memo 2 moves to the next question: **what is the stock worth?**

A company can be financially strong and still be a poor investment if the stock price is too high. A company can also face concerns but still be attractive if the market price already reflects those risks. Your task is to compare estimated value with market price and explain what the comparison suggests.

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> Valuation is not about finding one perfect price. It is about developing a defensible estimate and explaining the assumptions behind it.</div>

## Evidence to Include

Your memo should use selected valuation evidence. You do not need to include every calculation from your workbook. Use the evidence that matters most for the recommendation.

Possible evidence includes:

### Market Price

- Current stock price or recent market price
- Date of the observed price
- Ticker symbol and exchange

### Dividend-Based Valuation

- Current dividend
- Expected dividend growth
- Required return
- Dividend growth model estimate, if applicable

### Earnings Multiple Valuation

- Earnings per share
- Selected P/E multiple
- Comparable company or industry multiple
- Implied stock value

### Cash-Flow-Based Valuation

- Free cash flow estimate
- Growth assumption
- Discount rate
- Terminal growth assumption
- Estimated intrinsic value or enterprise value

### Valuation Range

- Low, base, and high valuation estimates
- Comparison with market price
- Margin of safety or lack of margin of safety

## Recommended Memo Structure

Use the following structure unless your instructor provides different directions.

### 1. Opening Recommendation

Begin with a clear valuation recommendation.

Examples:

- I would place this stock on a watchlist because the valuation appears close to fair value.
- I would not recommend purchase at the current price because valuation estimates fall below the market price.
- I would continue analyzing the stock because the base-case valuation suggests modest upside, but the conclusion is sensitive to growth assumptions.

The first paragraph should answer the main valuation question directly.

---

### 2. Company and Market Context

Briefly remind the reader what company is being analyzed and identify the current market price used in the analysis.

Include:

- Company name
- Ticker symbol
- Current or recent market price
- Date of the price observation
- One sentence connecting this memo to Memo 1

Keep this section concise. The memo should focus on valuation, not company background.

---

### 3. Valuation Evidence

Use at least two valuation approaches when possible.

Examples:

- Dividend growth model and P/E multiple
- P/E multiple and simple DCF
- DCF and comparable company multiple

Explain the result of each method and how the estimates compare with market price.

Weak version:

> The DDM value is $62.40 and the P/E value is $90.00.

Stronger version:

> The dividend growth model produces an estimated value below the current market price, suggesting the stock may be expensive if dividends are the primary valuation basis. The P/E multiple approach produces a value closer to the market price, which suggests the stock may be fairly valued if the selected multiple is reasonable.

---

### 4. Assumption Sensitivity

Identify which assumptions drive the valuation result.

Possible drivers include:

- Required return
- Growth rate
- Selected P/E multiple
- Free cash flow forecast
- Terminal growth rate
- Expected earnings growth

A strong memo explains whether the conclusion is robust or fragile.

---

### 5. Risk or Limitation

Acknowledge at least one important limitation.

Examples:

- The valuation depends heavily on assumed growth.
- The selected multiple may not reflect true peer comparability.
- A simplified DCF does not capture all future business risks.
- Market price may already reflect future expectations.
- Valuation methods disagree, suggesting uncertainty.

This section shows professional judgment.

---

### 6. Closing Decision

End by restating your valuation recommendation.

Possible decisions:

- Buy candidate
- Watchlist
- Avoid at current price
- Continue analysis with caution

## Sample Memo Excerpt

> I would place ExampleCo on a watchlist rather than recommend immediate purchase at the current price of $90. The valuation evidence is mixed. The dividend growth model estimates value at approximately $62.40, which suggests the stock may be expensive if dividends and long-run dividend growth are the primary basis for valuation. However, the P/E multiple approach estimates value near $90, which suggests the stock may be fairly priced if an 18x earnings multiple is reasonable. Because the methods disagree, I would not argue that the stock is clearly undervalued. The recommendation depends heavily on the required return, growth assumptions, and the selected P/E multiple. Based on the current valuation evidence, the stock appears closer to fairly valued or potentially overvalued than clearly attractive.

This excerpt is only a model. Your memo should use your own company, your own market price, and your own valuation evidence.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Making a valuation recommendation without comparing estimated value to market price
- Treating one valuation method as the final answer
- Using a P/E multiple without justification
- Using unrealistic growth assumptions
- Ignoring required return sensitivity
- Confusing a strong company with an undervalued stock
- Reporting model outputs without interpretation
- Forgetting to identify a limitation
- Making claims that your data do not support

---

## Professional Standard

<div class="callout-gold">
<strong>Memo 2 standard:</strong> A stock is not attractive simply because the company is strong. A stock is attractive only if the price is reasonable relative to expected value, risk, and assumptions.</div>
