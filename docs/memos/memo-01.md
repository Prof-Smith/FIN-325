## Purpose of Memo 1

Memo 1 asks you to diagnose whether a publicly traded company appears financially healthy enough to continue analyzing as a potential investment candidate.

This memo is the first step in your capstone investment recommendation. You are not yet making a final buy, sell, or hold recommendation. Instead, you are deciding whether the company is financially strong enough to deserve further analysis.

The goal is to move from calculation to judgment. A strong memo explains what the financial evidence suggests, what concerns remain, and whether the company should remain under consideration.

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> A company does not need to be perfect to remain an investment candidate, but the analyst must be clear about its financial strengths, weaknesses, and risks.</div>

## Main Decision Question

> **Does the company appear financially healthy enough to continue analyzing as a potential investment candidate?**

Your memo should answer this question clearly and directly.

## Required Company Selection

Choose one publicly traded company listed on the NYSE or NASDAQ.

Strong choices usually include companies with:

- Clear public financial statements
- Recent annual reports
- Sufficient ratio data
- Stable enough operations to interpret results
- Publicly available market and industry information

Examples include large companies such as Microsoft, Apple, Coca-Cola, Walmart, Nike, JPMorgan Chase, Costco, Disney, or Starbucks. You may choose another company if adequate information is available.

## Evidence to Include

Your memo should use selected evidence from your financial diagnosis. You do not need to include every ratio you calculated. Use the evidence that matters most.

Possible evidence includes:

### Liquidity

- Current ratio
- Quick ratio
- Ability to meet short-term obligations

### Profitability

- Net profit margin
- Return on assets
- Return on equity
- Operating performance

### Efficiency

- Asset turnover
- Ability to generate revenue from assets

### Leverage

- Debt-to-equity ratio
- Reliance on liabilities
- Financial risk

### DuPont Analysis

- Profit margin
- Asset turnover
- Equity multiplier
- Drivers of return on equity

## Recommended Memo Structure

Use the following structure unless your instructor provides different directions.

### 1. Opening Recommendation

Begin with a clear recommendation.

Examples:

- I would continue analyzing this company as a potential investment candidate because its profitability and liquidity appear strong.
- I would continue analyzing this company, but with caution because leverage appears elevated.
- I would not prioritize this company for further investment analysis because its financial condition appears weak relative to the risks identified.

The first paragraph should answer the main decision question directly.

---

### 2. Company Context

Briefly identify the company and explain why it is being analyzed.

Include:

- Company name
- Ticker symbol
- Industry or business description
- Why the company is relevant for analysis

Keep this section concise. The memo is not a company history report.

---

### 3. Financial Health Evidence

Use two or three of the most important financial indicators from your analysis.

You may discuss:

- Liquidity
- Profitability
- Efficiency
- Leverage
- DuPont drivers

Do not simply list ratios. Explain what the ratios suggest.

Weak version:

> The current ratio is 1.8 and ROE is 22%.

Stronger version:

> The company's liquidity appears adequate because current assets exceed current liabilities, while profitability appears strong based on return on equity. However, the DuPont analysis should be used to determine whether ROE is driven by operating performance or by leverage.

---

### 4. Main Concern or Limitation

Identify at least one concern or limitation.

Examples:

- Leverage may be high.
- Liquidity may be thin.
- Profitability may depend on unusual conditions.
- Ratio analysis does not determine whether the stock is fairly valued.
- A strong company can still be a poor investment if the price is too high.
- Industry comparison is needed before making a final recommendation.

This section shows judgment and intellectual honesty.

---

### 5. Closing Decision

End by restating your recommendation.

Your conclusion should make clear whether the company should continue into the next stage of analysis.

Possible decisions:

- Continue analysis
- Continue analysis with caution
- Do not prioritize for further analysis

## Sample Memo Excerpt

> I would continue analyzing Microsoft as a potential investment candidate because its financial health appears strong based on profitability, liquidity, and the quality of its return on equity. The company appears capable of generating substantial income relative to both revenue and equity, while its liquidity position suggests adequate ability to meet short-term obligations. The most important issue to examine further is valuation. A financially healthy company can still be unattractive if the market price already reflects overly optimistic expectations. Therefore, Microsoft appears financially strong enough to move forward into valuation analysis, but no final investment recommendation should be made until price, risk, and growth assumptions are evaluated.

This excerpt is only a model. Your memo should use your own company, your own evidence, and your own analysis.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing a company summary instead of a financial diagnosis
- Listing ratios without interpreting them
- Making a final buy recommendation too early
- Ignoring leverage or liquidity concerns
- Treating brand recognition as financial evidence
- Overstating conclusions from limited data
- Forgetting to identify a limitation
- Failing to answer the main decision question
