## Purpose of Memo 3

Memo 3 asks you to evaluate whether the company’s risk-return profile, cost of capital, and capital allocation discipline strengthen or weaken the investment case.

In Memo 1, you diagnosed the company’s financial health. In Memo 2, you evaluated whether the stock appeared undervalued, overvalued, or reasonably priced. Memo 3 moves deeper into the quality of the investment case by asking whether the company’s expected return, risk, cost of capital, and project discipline support the recommendation.

A company may look financially healthy and appear reasonably valued, but still be unattractive if its risk is too high, its cost of capital is elevated, or its capital allocation decisions do not create value.

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> A good investment case requires more than valuation upside. It also requires risk compensation, disciplined capital allocation, and evidence that management can earn returns above the cost of capital.</div>

## Main Decision Question

> **Does the company’s risk-return profile and capital allocation discipline strengthen or weaken your investment view?**

Your memo should answer this question clearly and directly.

## Evidence to Include

Your memo should use selected evidence from risk-return analysis, WACC estimation, and capital budgeting logic. You do not need to include every calculation. Use the evidence that matters most for your recommendation.

Possible evidence includes:

### Risk and Return

- Expected return
- Historical return
- Standard deviation
- Beta
- CAPM required return
- Comparison of expected return and required return

### Cost of Capital

- Cost of equity
- Pre-tax cost of debt
- After-tax cost of debt
- Capital structure weights
- WACC estimate
- Sensitivity to key assumptions

### Project Evaluation or Capital Allocation

- NPV
- IRR
- Payback period
- Scenario analysis
- Evidence of value-creating or value-destroying investment activity
- Management’s investment discipline

### Strategic Interpretation

- Does the firm appear to earn returns above its cost of capital?
- Does the risk level appear justified by expected return?
- Does the firm appear disciplined in capital allocation?
- Does the investment case improve or weaken after considering risk and WACC?

## Recommended Memo Structure

Use the following structure unless your instructor provides different directions.

### 1. Opening Recommendation

Begin with a clear recommendation about whether risk, cost of capital, and project evaluation strengthen or weaken the investment case.

Examples:

- The company’s risk-return profile strengthens the investment case because expected return appears to exceed the CAPM required return and WACC remains manageable.
- The investment case is weaker after considering risk because the expected return does not appear sufficient relative to beta and required return.
- I would keep the company on a watchlist because valuation appears reasonable, but project value creation depends heavily on optimistic cash flow assumptions.

The first paragraph should answer the main decision question directly.

---

### 2. Risk-Return Evidence

Discuss whether the expected return appears sufficient for the risk.

You may use:

- Beta
- CAPM required return
- Standard deviation
- Expected or historical return
- Comparison to market risk

Do not simply report beta. Explain what beta means for the recommendation.

Weak version:

> The beta is 1.2 and the required return is 11.2%.

Stronger version:

> The beta of 1.2 suggests above-market systematic risk, which raises the required return. If the stock’s expected return does not exceed the CAPM required return, the investment may not adequately compensate investors for risk.

---

### 3. Cost of Capital Evidence

Explain what WACC suggests about the company’s hurdle rate and investment discipline.

You may discuss:

- Estimated WACC
- Cost of equity
- After-tax cost of debt
- Capital structure weights
- Sensitivity to cost of equity or debt assumptions

A strong memo explains how WACC affects capital allocation.

---

### 4. Project or Capital Allocation Evidence

Use project evaluation or capital allocation evidence to assess whether the company appears to create value.

You may discuss:

- NPV
- IRR relative to WACC
- Scenario analysis
- Payback period
- Evidence that management invests above or below the cost of capital

This section should connect project-level evidence to the broader investment case.

---

### 5. Risk or Limitation

Acknowledge at least one important limitation.

Examples:

- Beta estimates may change over time.
- Historical returns may not predict future returns.
- WACC is an estimate, not a fact.
- Capital budgeting analysis depends on cash flow assumptions.
- Publicly available project information may be limited.
- A positive NPV project can still carry strategic or execution risk.

---

### 6. Closing Decision

End by clearly stating whether this stage of analysis strengthens, weakens, or complicates your investment view.

Possible conclusions:

- Strengthens investment case
- Weakens investment case
- Supports watchlist status
- Requires further analysis before final recommendation

## Sample Memo Excerpt

> The risk and cost of capital evidence weakens my investment view for ExampleCo. The company’s expected return is 11.0%, while CAPM implies a required return of approximately 11.2% based on a beta of 1.20, a 4.0% risk-free rate, and a 10.0% expected market return. This suggests the stock does not clearly compensate investors for systematic risk. The company’s estimated WACC of 9.1% provides a reasonable hurdle rate for average-risk projects, but capital budgeting evidence shows that project value creation is sensitive to base-case cash flow assumptions. A project with positive base-case NPV becomes unattractive in the downside case. Because both expected return and project value creation depend on assumptions that are not especially conservative, I would keep the company on a watchlist rather than strengthen the investment recommendation at this stage.

This excerpt is only a model. Your memo should use your own company, your own risk evidence, and your own cost of capital or project analysis.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Discussing return without discussing risk
- Treating beta as the only measure of risk
- Using CAPM without interpreting the required return
- Calculating WACC without explaining what it means
- Treating WACC as a perfect number
- Using IRR without comparing it to WACC
- Ignoring downside scenarios
- Making a recommendation without connecting risk, cost of capital, and capital allocation
- Forgetting to identify a limitation
