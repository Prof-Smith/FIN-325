## Welcome to Module 6

In Module 5, you examined how risk affects required return. Module 6 applies that logic to firms.

Companies finance themselves using a mix of equity and debt. Each source of capital has a cost. Equity investors require compensation for ownership risk. Debt investors require compensation for lending risk. The firm's weighted average cost of capital, or WACC, combines these required returns into one estimate that can be used to evaluate average-risk investment projects.

By the end of this module, you will estimate a firm's WACC and explain how that cost of capital should be used as a hurdle rate for investment decisions.

## Why This Module Matters

The cost of capital is one of the most important concepts in corporate finance because it links financing decisions to investment decisions.

Managers and analysts use WACC to answer questions such as:

- What return must the firm earn to create value?
- How much does equity capital cost?
- How much does debt capital cost after taxes?
- How does capital structure affect the overall cost of capital?
- What hurdle rate should be used for average-risk projects?
- When is WACC not the right discount rate?

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> A project does not create value simply because it is profitable. It creates value only if it earns more than the capital required to fund it.
</div>

## What You Will Learn

During this module, we will focus on five major ideas.

### 1. Cost of Equity

You will estimate the return required by shareholders, often using risk-return logic such as CAPM.

### 2. Cost of Debt

You will estimate the return required by lenders and understand why debt is usually measured after tax in WACC.

### 3. Capital Structure Weights

You will combine debt and equity using appropriate weights.

### 4. WACC

You will calculate the weighted average cost of capital.

### 5. Hurdle Rate Judgment

You will explain when WACC is appropriate and when project-specific risk may require adjustment.
