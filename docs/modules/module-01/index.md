## Welcome to Module 1

Every financial decision ultimately asks one core question:

> **Does this decision create value?**

Before you can value a stock, analyze a project, estimate the cost of capital, or make an investment recommendation, you first need to understand what financial managers do and how companies are evaluated.

This module introduces the role of finance in business decision-making and gives you the foundation for analyzing whether a company appears financially healthy.

By the end of this module, you will complete your first applied finance lab by evaluating a real publicly traded company using financial ratios, DuPont analysis, and professional judgment.

---

## Essential Question

> **How do financial managers create value, and how can we determine whether a company is financially healthy?**

This question will guide your work throughout Module 1. You will begin by learning how financial managers think about value, markets, incentives, governance, and performance. You will then apply those ideas to a real company.

## Why This Module Matters

Financial statement analysis is one of the most practical skills in business. It is used by:

- Financial analysts
- Credit analysts
- Commercial bankers
- Investment analysts
- Corporate finance professionals
- Managers evaluating business performance

A company can be growing and still be financially weak. A company can be profitable and still face liquidity concerns. A company can report strong earnings while relying heavily on leverage.

Your task in Module 1 is to begin seeing beyond the surface-level story.

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> Financial analysis is not just about calculating ratios. It is about using ratios to understand the economic story of a company.
</div>

## What You Will Learn

During this module, we will focus on four major areas.

### 1. The Role of Financial Management

You will examine how financial managers help organizations make decisions about:

- Investment
- Financing
- Risk
- Capital allocation
- Value creation

### 2. The Financial Market Environment

You will consider how firms interact with:

- Investors
- Financial institutions
- Capital markets
- Regulators
- Stakeholders

### 3. Financial Statement Analysis

You will learn how to evaluate firm performance using:

- Income statements
- Balance sheets
- Statements of cash flows
- Financial ratios

### 4. Managerial Judgment

You will begin developing the ability to move from calculation to interpretation.

A ratio by itself is not enough. A strong analyst asks:

- What does this number mean?
- Is this number improving or deteriorating?
- What is driving the result?
- What risks does this reveal?
- What decision should follow?
