## Welcome to Module 2

Finance begins with a simple but powerful idea:

> **A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow.**

This module introduces the time value of money, one of the central concepts in finance. Nearly every major financial decision depends on comparing cash flows that occur at different points in time. Whether a manager is evaluating a project, an investor is valuing a bond, a family is planning for retirement, or a company is deciding whether to borrow or invest, the same logic applies.

In Module 1, you learned how to diagnose the financial health of a company. In Module 2, you begin learning how to evaluate the value of money over time.

By the end of this module, you will build a time value of money model in Excel and use it to evaluate a financial decision under different assumptions.

## Why This Module Matters

The time value of money is not just a formula. It is the logic behind valuation.

Managers and analysts use time value of money to answer questions such as:

- What is a future payment worth today?
- How much will an investment grow over time?
- What interest rate is implied by a financial decision?
- How much should be invested today to reach a future goal?
- Which cash flow stream is more valuable?
- How sensitive is value to changes in assumptions?

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> Time value of money is the bridge between accounting numbers and financial valuation. It teaches you how to compare cash flows across time.
</div>

## What You Will Learn

During this module, we will focus on five core ideas.

### 1. Future Value

You will estimate how much an investment grows over time when interest or return compounds.

### 2. Present Value

You will discount future cash flows back to today's value.

### 3. Annuities

You will value repeated cash flows, such as loan payments, retirement contributions, lease payments, or bond coupons.

### 4. Interest Rates and Time Horizons

You will examine how small changes in assumptions can produce large changes in value.

### 5. Decision-Making Under Assumptions

You will learn that financial models depend on assumptions. A strong analyst does not just calculate one answer. A strong analyst tests how the answer changes when assumptions change.

## Step 1: Understand the Scenario

You will evaluate a financial decision involving cash flows across time. Your instructor may assign a scenario, or you may choose from examples such as:

- Saving for a future financial goal
- Comparing two investment alternatives
- Evaluating a loan payment structure
- Estimating the present value of future payments
- Comparing a lump sum with a series of payments

The key requirement is that the decision must involve cash flows occurring at different points in time.

---

## Step 2: Build

Create an Excel workbook that includes:

- Clear input assumptions
- Present value calculation
- Future value calculation
- Annuity calculation, if applicable
- Sensitivity analysis using at least two discount rates or return assumptions
- A summary output section

Your model should make it easy to answer: **What happens if the interest rate or time horizon changes?**

---

## Step 3: Analyze

After completing the model, interpret the results.

Your analysis should address:

1. What is the present value of the future cash flow stream?
2. What is the future value of the current investment or payment stream?
3. How sensitive is the result to the interest rate or discount rate?
4. How does the time horizon affect the outcome?
5. Which assumption matters most?

Do not simply report the spreadsheet output. Explain what the output means for the decision.

---

## Step 4: Decide

Write a short decision memo of approximately 250-500 words.

Answer the following question:

> **Based on your time value of money analysis, which financial choice creates more value?**

Your recommendation should be clear, evidence-based, and professional.

A strong recommendation will:

- State the financial choice clearly
- Use present value or future value evidence
- Explain the importance of assumptions
- Discuss at least one limitation
- Make a defensible decision

## What Strong Work Looks Like

Strong work does more than calculate one present value or future value.

A strong submission will explain how the decision changes when assumptions change. For example:

> The investment appears attractive under the base-case return assumption because the future value exceeds the target amount. However, the result is highly sensitive to the assumed annual return. Under the lower-return scenario, the investment falls short of the goal, which suggests that the decision depends heavily on whether the assumed return is realistic.

That kind of answer shows that you understand both the calculation and the uncertainty behind the calculation.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid these common problems:

- Confusing present value and future value
- Using the wrong sign convention in Excel
- Forgetting to match rate and period assumptions
- Treating the discount rate as certain
- Reporting spreadsheet output without interpreting it
- Ignoring the effect of time horizon
- Making a recommendation without discussing assumptions
