## Welcome to Module 3

In Module 2, you learned how to compare cash flows across time. Module 3 applies that same logic to bonds.

A bond is a promise to make future payments. The value of a bond depends on the size of those payments, when those payments occur, and the return investors require for bearing the bond's risk.

This module introduces bond valuation, yield to maturity, coupon structure, premium and discount bonds, and interest rate sensitivity.

By the end of this module, you will build a bond valuation model and use it to make a recommendation about whether a bond appears attractively priced.

> **How do interest rates, coupon payments, and maturity affect the value and risk of a bond?**

This question will guide your work throughout Module 3. You will learn how to price a bond, interpret why bond prices move, and evaluate whether a bond is attractive relative to an investor's required return.

## Why This Module Matters

Bond valuation is central to corporate finance and investment decision-making. Companies issue bonds to raise capital, investors buy bonds for income and diversification, and interest rate changes affect the value of debt securities across financial markets.

Managers and analysts use bond valuation to answer questions such as:

- What is a bond worth today?
- Why do bond prices fall when yields rise?
- Is a bond trading at a premium or discount?
- How does maturity affect interest rate sensitivity?
- How does coupon structure affect bond risk?
- Is the offered market price attractive relative to required return?

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> A bond price is not just a number. It reflects the market's required return for receiving a sequence of promised future cash flows.
</div>

## What You Will Learn

During this module, we will focus on five major ideas.

### 1. Bond Cash Flows

You will identify the coupon payments and face value repayment that create a bond's value.

### 2. Bond Pricing

You will estimate bond value by discounting coupon payments and face value back to the present.

### 3. Yield to Maturity

You will interpret yield to maturity as the return implied by the bond's price and promised cash flows.

### 4. Premium and Discount Bonds

You will explain why bonds sell above or below face value when coupon rates differ from required yields.

### 5. Interest Rate Sensitivity

You will examine why bond prices move inversely with yields and why longer-maturity bonds are often more sensitive to rate changes.

## Bond Valuation: Is This Bond Attractively Priced?

In Lab 3, you will value a bond and decide whether it appears attractively priced relative to the investor's required yield.

This lab asks you to move beyond formula recognition. Your goal is to understand how bond price, coupon rate, yield, maturity, and interest rate sensitivity interact.

## Step 1: Understand the Bond

You will evaluate a bond using information such as:

- Face value
- Coupon rate
- Years to maturity
- Payment frequency
- Yield to maturity
- Market price

The key requirement is that the bond must have a clear set of promised cash flows.

---

## Step 2: Build

Create an Excel workbook that includes:

- Bond input section
- Coupon payment calculation
- Number of periods
- Periodic yield
- Present value of coupon payments
- Present value of face value
- Estimated bond price
- Premium or discount status
- Price/yield sensitivity analysis
- Summary output section

Your model should make it easy to answer: **What happens if the required yield changes?**

---

## Step 3: Analyze

After completing the model, interpret the results.

Your analysis should address:

1. Is the bond priced at a premium, discount, or near par?
2. Why does the bond trade above or below face value?
3. How does the coupon rate compare with the yield to maturity?
4. How sensitive is the bond price to interest rate changes?
5. How does maturity affect risk?
6. What would concern an investor most?

Do not simply report the spreadsheet output. Explain what the output means for the decision.

---

## Step 4: Decide

Write a short bond recommendation memo of approximately 250-500 words.

Answer the following question:

> **Based on your bond valuation analysis, would you consider purchasing this bond? Why or why not?**

Your recommendation should be clear, evidence-based, and professional.

A strong recommendation will:

- State the financial choice clearly
- Use bond pricing evidence
- Explain premium or discount status
- Discuss interest rate risk
- Acknowledge at least one limitation
- Make a defensible decision

## What Strong Work Looks Like

Strong work does more than calculate a bond price.

A strong submission will explain why the bond trades at a premium or discount and how the recommendation would change if yields rise or fall. For example:

> The bond trades at a discount because its coupon rate is lower than the investor's required yield. At the base-case yield, the estimated value is close to the market price, so the bond does not appear meaningfully underpriced. However, the bond has interest rate risk because a rise in yields would reduce its value.

That kind of answer shows that you understand both the calculation and the risk behind the calculation.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid these common problems:

- Forgetting to adjust coupon payments for semiannual periods
- Forgetting to adjust yield for semiannual periods
- Confusing coupon rate with yield to maturity
- Treating face value as the current market price
- Reporting price without explaining premium or discount status
- Ignoring interest rate sensitivity
- Making a recommendation without discussing risk
