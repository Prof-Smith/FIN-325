## Welcome to Module 4

In Module 3, you valued bonds by discounting promised future cash flows. Module 4 applies valuation logic to stocks, where the future is less certain and assumptions matter more.

Equity valuation is one of the most important skills in finance because it forces analysts to connect company performance, growth expectations, risk, and market price. A stock is not attractive simply because the company is strong. A strong company can still be overpriced. A weaker company can sometimes be undervalued if expectations are too low.

By the end of this module, you will build an equity valuation model and use it to make a recommendation about whether a stock appears undervalued, overvalued, or reasonably priced.

---

## Essential Question

> **How do analysts estimate the value of a stock when future cash flows, growth, and risk are uncertain?**

This question will guide your work throughout Module 4. You will compare multiple valuation approaches and learn why professional analysts often think in valuation ranges rather than single-point estimates.

## Why This Module Matters

Equity valuation sits at the center of investment analysis, corporate finance, and strategic decision-making.

Analysts use equity valuation to answer questions such as:

- What is a reasonable estimate of intrinsic value?
- Is the current market price attractive?
- Which assumptions matter most?
- Is the stock undervalued, overvalued, or fairly priced?
- How should different valuation methods be reconciled?
- What risks could make the valuation wrong?

<div class="callout-finance">
<strong>Professional insight:</strong> Equity valuation is not about finding one perfect price. It is about developing a defensible estimate under uncertainty.
</div>

## What You Will Learn

During this module, we will focus on five major ideas.

### 1. Intrinsic Value

You will examine the idea that a stock's value should reflect expected future benefits to shareholders.

### 2. Dividend Growth Valuation

You will estimate value for dividend-paying firms using expected dividends, required return, and growth.

### 3. Relative Valuation

You will use earnings multiples, such as P/E, to estimate value based on comparable market pricing.

### 4. Free Cash Flow Valuation

You will explore how cash-flow-based valuation links operating performance, growth, discount rates, and terminal value.

### 5. Valuation Judgment

You will learn why valuation models are assumption-sensitive and why strong recommendations acknowledge uncertainty.

## Applied Finance Lab 4

## Equity Valuation: Is This Stock Attractively Priced?

In Lab 4, you will value a stock and decide whether it appears undervalued, overvalued, or reasonably priced.

This lab asks you to move beyond formula recognition. Your goal is to understand how dividends, earnings, cash flows, growth, risk, and market price interact.

[Open Lab 4: Equity Valuation](../../labs/lab-04/index.md)

---

## Step 1: Select a Company

Choose one publicly traded company listed on the NYSE or NASDAQ.

Your company should have enough available information to support at least two valuation approaches, such as:

- Dividend growth model
- P/E multiple valuation
- Simple free cash flow valuation
- Comparable company multiple

The key requirement is that you can explain why the valuation methods you choose are appropriate for the company.

---

## Step 2: Build

Create an Excel workbook that includes:

- Company input section
- Current market price
- Key financial inputs
- At least two valuation methods
- Sensitivity analysis
- Summary valuation range
- Professional formatting

Your model should make it easy to answer: **What happens if the assumptions change?**

---

## Step 3: Analyze

After completing the valuation model, interpret the results.

Your analysis should address:

1. What valuation methods did you use?
2. Why are those methods appropriate for this company?
3. What estimated value did each method produce?
4. How do those values compare with the current market price?
5. Which assumptions drive the valuation most?
6. Does the stock appear undervalued, overvalued, or reasonably priced?
7. What would make your recommendation wrong?

Do not simply report model outputs. Explain the story behind the valuation.

---

## Step 4: Decide

Write a short valuation memo of approximately 250-500 words.

Answer the following question:

> **Based on your equity valuation analysis, would you buy, avoid, or place this stock on a watchlist? Why?**

Your recommendation should be clear, evidence-based, and professional.

A strong recommendation will:

- State a clear position
- Use valuation evidence
- Explain assumptions
- Include sensitivity insight
- Acknowledge at least one limitation
- Make a defensible decision

## What Strong Work Looks Like

Strong work does more than produce an estimated stock price.

A strong submission will explain why different methods may produce different values and what assumptions drive the recommendation. For example:

> The dividend model suggests the stock is expensive relative to expected dividend growth, while the P/E multiple approach suggests the stock is close to fairly valued. Because the valuation range is sensitive to growth and required return assumptions, I would place the stock on a watchlist rather than recommend an immediate purchase.

That kind of answer shows that you understand valuation as judgment under uncertainty.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid these common problems:

- Treating one valuation method as the final answer
- Ignoring assumptions
- Using unrealistic growth rates
- Applying a P/E multiple without justification
- Comparing intrinsic value to market price without explaining the gap
- Forgetting that a strong company can still be overpriced
- Making a recommendation without sensitivity analysis
