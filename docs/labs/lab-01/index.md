## Lab Objective

Use financial statement analysis to evaluate whether a publicly traded company appears financially healthy.

This lab is not just a calculation exercise. Your goal is to use financial evidence to tell a clear story about a company's performance, risk, and financial position.

## Analyst Scenario

You are preparing a short financial diagnosis for someone considering whether to invest in a publicly traded company.

The investor does not want a long report. The investor wants a clear judgment supported by evidence.

Your task is to answer this question:

> **Based solely on financial statement analysis, does this company appear financially healthy enough to consider as an investment?**

## Step 1: Select a Company

Choose a publicly traded company listed on the NYSE or NASDAQ.

Strong choices include firms with clear financial statements and broad public coverage, such as:

- Microsoft
- Apple
- Nike
- Starbucks
- JPMorgan Chase
- Coca-Cola
- Walmart
- Disney
- Tesla
- Costco

You may select another company if enough financial data is available.

!!! warning "Avoid weak company choices"
    Avoid companies with incomplete data, extremely unusual accounting structures, or insufficient public financial information unless approved by the instructor.

---

## Step 2: Gather Financial Statement Data

Use the company's most recent annual report, 10-K, or investor relations materials.

You will need enough information to calculate:

- Current assets
- Current liabilities
- Cash and equivalents, if available
- Inventory, if applicable
- Total assets
- Total liabilities
- Total equity
- Revenue
- Net income

Document the year of the financial statements used.

---

## Step 3: Build the Ratio Analysis Workbook

Create a clean Excel workbook with the following sections.

### Section 1: Company Information

Include:

- Company name
- Ticker symbol
- Fiscal year analyzed
- Data source

### Section 2: Financial Statement Inputs

Organize the financial statement values used in your calculations.

Your inputs should be clearly labeled and easy to audit.

### Section 3: Ratio Calculations

Calculate the following ratios.

| Category | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Current ratio |
| Liquidity | Quick ratio |
| Profitability | Net profit margin |
| Profitability | Return on assets |
| Profitability | Return on equity |
| Efficiency | Asset turnover |
| Leverage | Debt-to-equity ratio |

### Section 4: DuPont Analysis

Use the DuPont framework:

```text
ROE = Profit Margin x Asset Turnover x Equity Multiplier
```

This analysis helps identify whether ROE is driven primarily by profitability, efficiency, or leverage.

---

## Step 4: Analyze the Results

In your written analysis, answer the following questions.

### Financial Strengths

1. What is the company's strongest financial characteristic?
2. Which ratio best supports that conclusion?

### Financial Weaknesses

3. What is the company's greatest financial concern?
4. Which ratio best supports that concern?

### ROE Drivers

5. What appears to be driving return on equity?
6. Is ROE supported by profitability, efficiency, leverage, or some combination?

### Risk

7. Does leverage appear to create meaningful financial risk?
8. Would the company look stronger or weaker without the effect of leverage?

### Decision Relevance

9. What would an investor, lender, or manager care about most?

---

## Step 5: Decide

Write a 250-500 word recommendation that answers:

> **Based solely on your financial statement analysis, would you invest in this company? Why or why not?**

Your recommendation should be written in a professional tone and supported by evidence from your ratios.

A strong recommendation includes:

- A clear yes, no, or cautious/conditional recommendation
- Specific financial evidence
- Interpretation of the evidence
- A discussion of at least one limitation
- Professional judgment

## Example Recommendation Structure

You may organize your recommendation using this structure:

```text
Recommendation: I would / would not / would cautiously consider investing in [Company].

Evidence: The strongest evidence is...

Interpretation: This suggests...

Concern or limitation: However...

Conclusion: Therefore...
```

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Calculating ratios incorrectly
- Using one ratio to make the entire decision
- Ignoring leverage
- Reporting numbers without explaining them
- Making claims that are not supported by your analysis
- Submitting a workbook that is difficult to follow
- Forgetting that ratio analysis is useful but incomplete
